Selected Project

Shipped

Eight signatures, one branch visit

Vietnam's oldest consumer finance company originated every loan in-branch. It became a mobile app for applying, signing and servicing a loan without visiting one.

Outcome

5h → 2hApproval time (target)
8 → 1Signatures (client-reported)

Problem

HD Saison originated 100% of loans in-branch — the only lender in its five-company peer set with no digital path at all, while rivals ran 66–86% offline and the market grew 23.7% a year. Borrowing meant a 3–5 hour visit and eight separate signatures.

I reframed a four-goal client brief around one problem — get origination off the counter — and sequenced OTP e-signature first, because nothing downstream works while a branch visit exists to collect eight wet signatures. What I killed was remarketing, ranked equally by the client. The app shipped in May 2022 and is still live four years on.

A chain that locked developers out

A permissioned NFT chain with a closed developer pool became a permissionless, EVM-compatible Layer 1 open to any Ethereum-toolchain builder.

Outcome

14.5M+Smart contracts (published)
141K+Active addresses (published)

Problem

Aura ran as a permissioned CosmWasm chain built for NFTs. Onboarding a team meant teaching them an unfamiliar stack, behind an approval gate — so the addressable developer pool was a fraction of the EVM world's.

I chose a dual-environment upgrade over a full migration: EVM alongside CosmWasm, widening the developer funnel without breaking the dApps already live. The price was a known address seam I shipped visibly rather than hold the release for. What I killed to pay for it was the NFT-first consumer roadmap.

Seventeen roles, one release

Pharma speaker programs ran on email, spreadsheets and a legacy portal. They became one multi-tenant platform where a rep, a compliance monitor and an accountant work the same program record.

Outcome

3 → 1Systems reconciled (target)
17Roles on one system (client-reported)

Problem

Seventeen roles touch a single speaker program — rep, manager, speaker, coordinator, compliance, accounting, auditor — each with its own approval gate and its own definition of "done". Mapped end to end, that came to 496 user stories across 13 workflow areas, all of it competing for one MVP.

I cut the backlog to the program-request spine — the one flow all 17 roles touch — and shipped the four roles that own it end to end. What I deferred was every role that only reads the record: attendee self-service, auditor tooling, the rookie onboarding track.

Cutting four roles to keep one release

Customer Care planning ran on an old internal tool that couldn't connect to the rest of the ecosystem — redundant fields on screen and no way to capture what a customer said while a rep was travelling or mid-visit.

Outcome

6.2 → 3.6 minTime per request, −42% (estimated)
36Usability defects fixed (estimated)

Problem

The planning tool was old, hard to use, and couldn't integrate with the rest of the stack. Thirty-six usability defects were logged across the team, and nothing supported staff capturing notes on the move.

I cut four of the five user roles the redesign was trying to serve and rebuilt around the one screen managers actually planned trips from. It shipped in six weeks and is now folded into the company super app.

Eight apps, one team

A startup was building eight apps at once for three user types. It became one transaction spine — book, check in, pay — with everything else sequenced behind it.

Outcome

8 → 1Parallel apps unified (target)
50Active salons (client-reported)

Problem

iSystem wanted 20% of a 54,000-salon US market and had 50 salons on the platform — a 220x gap. One team was scoped to build eight apps in parallel, with four going to staff and owners while customers waited on the other four.

I sequenced the build around the only order the eight apps actually support: check-in is meaningless without a booking, the POS has nothing to total without a check-in. What I killed was the remarketing suite — the client's own stated priority. iSystem folded before general availability, so no launch number here is mine to claim.
